CityMoods

Data downloads

Every day, CityMoods sorts each city's 311 requests into the same ten moods, so you can compare rats in Chicago with rodents in New York. Here's all of it as plain CSV files, free to use.

Updated nightly (newest day: 2026-11-20). Free to use with credit under CC BY 4.0: please cite "CityMoods (citymoods311.com), from [city] 311 open data".

What you can do with it

  • Spot a spike. San Francisco's storm on Wednesday, Nov 18 brought 677 street flooding reports, against a usual 3. In any city's citywide file, compare requests with usual.
  • Find where it happened. The neighborhood files showed that 34 of New York's 52 "alarm that won't stop" reports that day came from Long Island City–Hunters Point.
  • Compare cities. The all-cities file puts the five side by side, say no-heat complaints in New York and Boston as winter sets in.
  • Track a trend. Every day is kept, so you can follow rats, flooding or streetlights week by week.

How to start

  1. Download a CSV below and open it in Google Sheets or Excel. Unsure what a column means? See the column definitions.
  2. Filter by mood or neighborhood.
  3. Compare requests with usual. Anything around 2× or more is a spike worth a look.

Analyze it with AI

Type a question, copy the prompt, and paste it into Claude, ChatGPT or any AI assistant that can read web pages. It pulls the numbers straight from these files, down to each neighborhood's top request types, which makes it useful for newsrooms, researchers, real estate and legal teams.

How it's made, and what's left out


    
  
  • Each night we pull the most recent complete day from each city's open data portal. Cities publish on their own schedules, so the newest day is usually one or two days back.
    • 
  
  • Only request types, their fixed-menu details and neighborhoods are used. No addresses, coordinates, names or written notes are downloaded or published. (NYC requests are placed in a neighborhood from their rough location, which is then discarded.)
    • 
  
  • Neighborhood counts under 3 are never published, so neighborhood rows don't always add up to the citywide totals.
    • 
  
  • Requests about people (homelessness, panhandling, police matters and similar) are counted in "Everything else" and never named.
    • 
  
  • Chicago's information-only calls and O'Hare airport noise complaints are left out of the neighborhood counts.
    • 
  
  • Need individual records? Use the official portal linked under each city.
    •
