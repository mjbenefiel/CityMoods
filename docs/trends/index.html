What Chicago reports, neighborhood by neighborhood.

See which 311 requests come up most in each Chicago neighborhood, and how neighborhoods compare, over the past week up to the past year. Handy if you're curious about your own neighborhood, getting to know a new one, or reporting a story.

  1. Pick what to look at. A request type (rats, potholes, tree trims) or a whole mood, and a time range.
  2. See where it comes up. Darker neighborhoods on the map had more reports; the list ranks them. Switch to "per 1,000 residents" to even out big and small neighborhoods.
  3. Compare and explore. Tap up to 3 neighborhoods for side-by-side numbers, or search for yours (by name, address or location) to open its full profile: what it asks 311 for most, what stands out, and a year-long trend.

      What this shows: how often people asked the city for something, not how good a neighborhood is. Some neighborhoods call 311 more than others for the same problem, and big neighborhoods get more reports simply because more people live there. "Per 1,000 residents" evens that out.

      Counts come from the City of Chicago's 311 open data, counted by community area on the city's portal: no individual requests, addresses or names are downloaded. Counts under 3 are hidden. Requests about people, such as wage or housing complaints, aren't included. Population: U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey 5-year estimates. "Time to close" and the advisors' close times use requests made in the year ending a month before the latest data, so each had at least a month; they count requests the city marked completed, and a neighborhood needs 20+ requests of a type to show a share. Want the numbers? See the Data page.

      Data: Chicago 311 Service Requests and ACS 5-year data by community area, from the City of Chicago Data Portal. CityMoods is independent and not affiliated with the City of Chicago.
